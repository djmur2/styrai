# StyrAI Ninety-Day Validation Plan

The goal is not to prove that AI governance matters. The goal is to prove that a
specific buyer will pay StyrAI to control and evidence a specific agent
workflow.

## Days 1–21 — problem evidence

Conduct 12–15 structured conversations with AI, security, quality, risk, privacy
or technology leaders. Split the first pass between financial services and life
sciences/diagnostics, then select one vertical before building a real connector.

Ask for recent events and artefacts rather than opinions:

- Which agents or agent-like automations are already being tested?
- What real action is the organisation unwilling to let an agent perform alone?
- How is agent identity and delegated human authority represented today?
- Where is approval evidence stored?
- Can the team reconstruct which policy was active for an earlier action?
- What has delayed a deployment or triggered security concern?
- Who owns the budget for solving this?

For financial services also ask:

- Which GenAI or agent use cases sit outside the traditional model inventory?
- Who approves a new model version, provider or intended use?
- Can the team link a model's validation status to a specific runtime action?
- How are four-eyes controls and actual execution outcomes evidenced today?
- Which AI or model providers appear in the DORA third-party register?

Evidence gate:

- at least five buyers describe the same consequential workflow problem;
- at least three agree to a technical follow-up using a real workflow;
- at least one has a credible budget owner and purchasing path.

Choose the first vertical using paid commitment, buyer access and integration
feasibility—not the apparent size of the total market.

If this gate fails, narrow the segment or problem before expanding the product.

## Days 22–45 — concierge demonstration

Use the governed-action vertical slice to demonstrate:

- explicit allow;
- explicit deny;
- human approval;
- fail-closed handling;
- verifiable action evidence.

Configure demonstrations from the interviewees' workflow descriptions. Keep the
tool execution simulated until data handling and security have been agreed.

Evidence gate:

- three prospects ask to test their own workflow;
- two provide sample rules or scenarios;
- one accepts a written paid-pilot proposal.

## Days 46–75 — paid design partner

Integrate one test workflow. Measure installation time, decision latency, approval
completion, evidence completeness and reviewer reconstruction time.

Evidence gate:

- a signed paid engagement or equivalent purchasing commitment;
- a named business owner and risk owner;
- agreed conversion criteria before implementation starts.

## Days 76–90 — investment decision

Continue product investment only if the design partner uses the control in a
representative workflow and agrees that continued operation has budgetable value.

Choose one outcome:

1. **Product:** repeatable need, paid pilot, credible recurring conversion.
2. **Productised service:** demand exists but integrations remain highly bespoke.
3. **Narrow or stop:** interest is general, no workflow owner pays, or existing
   platforms already solve the customer's practical problem.

## Weekly scorecard

- qualified conversations completed;
- repeated workflow problems observed;
- technical follow-ups secured;
- pilot proposals issued;
- paid commitments;
- product assumptions invalidated;
- time spent building versus speaking with buyers.
