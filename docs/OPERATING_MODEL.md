# StyrAI Operating Model

## Principle

Codex can build and maintain much of the operating machinery, but customer trust,
legal accountability and consequential approvals remain human responsibilities.

## Founder responsibilities

- choose the target segment and approve commercial positioning;
- conduct or attend customer discovery and sales conversations;
- approve pricing, contracts, external communications and spending;
- obtain professional legal, privacy, security and regulatory advice;
- approve any access to customer systems or data;
- own product-risk decisions and high-impact customer commitments.

## Codex responsibilities

- maintain the product brief, architecture, backlog and decision record;
- implement, test, document and review the product;
- convert interview notes into evidence-backed patterns and requirements;
- draft pilot proposals, demonstrations and customer documentation;
- create repeatable validation, onboarding, testing and release workflows;
- keep claims tied to evidence and flag assumptions that remain unvalidated;
- automate low-risk internal processes after the founder approves the workflow.

## Workstreams

1. **Market evidence** — interviews, competitor tracking and willingness to pay.
2. **Product** — governed-action gateway, approvals, evidence and connectors.
3. **Trust** — threat model, privacy design, security tests and control mappings.
4. **Commercial** — pilot offer, proposals, pricing experiments and pipeline.
5. **Operations** — decisions, metrics, releases and customer support.

## Decision discipline

Every material feature should link to at least one of:

- an observed customer problem;
- a signed pilot requirement;
- a security or reliability requirement;
- a clearly identified regulatory or contractual obligation.

Ideas without such evidence stay in discovery and do not enter the build queue.

## Automation boundary

Safe candidates for automation include tests, documentation checks, evidence-pack
generation, release preparation, research monitoring and internal scorecards.

External sales messages, contractual claims, production permissions, customer
data access and final high-risk action approvals require explicit human review.

## Initial cadence

- weekly: customer evidence and assumption review;
- weekly: product demonstration against current acceptance tests;
- fortnightly: security and architecture review;
- monthly: continue, narrow, productise as a service, or stop decision.

Regulatory monitoring creates a reviewed change proposal. It must never change a
customer's production policy automatically. A human owner validates the source,
legal interpretation, affected customers, test evidence and release decision.
